CRAG uses a retrieval evaluator to assess the quality of retrieved documents 
and routes the pipeline into different actions (CORRECT, AMBIGUOUS, INCORRECT) 
based on threshold rules.

During implementation, I observed that the routing decision is driven by the per-document 
relevance scores. A single highly relevant chunk can dominate the decision even when the 
remaining retrieved evidence is weak or inconsistent.

I introduced a ConfidenceReport that summarizes retrieval quality using multiple statistics 
instead of relying solely on individual relevance scores.

The idea was inspired by a general principle in Information Retrieval: retrieval quality 
should be assessed using the overall evidence rather than a single score. Similar feature 
aggregation is commonly used in ranking systems and confidence estimation, so I applied the 
same principle to CRAG's routing stage.
